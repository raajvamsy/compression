FunnelZip · can it make money?

Not as a product. Maybe as a cost cutter.

Streaming, PaaS and consumer storage don't fit. One business does: cutting a company's cloud storage bill for data that has to stay exact (logs, sensor data, audio masters, scientific and medical images). You'd charge a share of what they save. It's a known business model, it needs proof on real customer data, and the speed has to improve first.

The short answer

What has been sold before, and what we have

Nobody has built a big business by selling compression to the public. The money has gone to companies that store huge amounts of data. Dropbox built Lepton (lossless JPEG, ~22% smaller) and saved 46 PiB on 150 billion files. Granica raised $45M to cut AI training data in S3 by up to 80%, and charges a share of the savings. FunnelZip's best results are on exactly that kind of data: sensor logs −52%, server logs −30%, audio −44% vs FLAC. But it runs at about 1 MB/s, while Lepton does 5 MB/s compress and 15 MB/s decompress.

If you want to try

A 30-day test before anyone spends money

Pick one kind of data where we're far ahead

Logs and telemetry (−30% to −52% vs the best tool), or audio archives such as broadcast, podcast and music masters (−44% vs FLAC). Not photos: JPEG XL is free and close behind.

Benchmark against the strongest tools, not just the common ones

Logs: zstd with a trained dictionary, and xz. Audio: OptimFROG and Monkey's Audio Extra High, not only FLAC. If the lead shrinks below ~15%, stop.

Make it 5–10× faster in that one niche

Use only the lenses that matter for that data type, split big files into parts that really differ (as we did for JPEG planes), and let the decoder read any chunk on its own. Below ~5 MB/s, the compute bill eats the savings on hot data.

Package it as a drop-in S3 gateway

Compress on write, decompress on read, the same API, and the customer changes nothing. Use outcome pricing: a share of the storage bill saved, as Granica does.

Find one design partner with ≥100 TB of that data

Run it on their real data and measure the real dollars saved. That one number decides whether this is a business.

Risks, honestly

What could stop it

riskwhy it matters
Speed~1 MB/s per core, and decompress is just as slow. Dropbox required 15 MB/s decode so users never waited. Hot data and streaming are out until this improves 10×.
Free competitorsJPEG XL repacks JPEGs ~20% losslessly for free, and zstd, xz and FLAC are everywhere. Our lead has to be big enough to pay for switching.
Cheap cold storageDeep Archive is ~$1/TB/month. Saving 25% of $1 rarely pays for the compute. Only hot or warm storage ($4–23/TB) leaves real margin.
TrustStorage buyers fear losing data more than they want savings. A new format needs a hardened decoder, fuzzing, versioning and years of zero-loss history. The lossless proof is a good start, not enough.
Not patentable as a wholeContext mixing is old, public prior art (PAQ, cmix, Lepton). The moat would be the product, the niche data and the customers, not the algorithm.
